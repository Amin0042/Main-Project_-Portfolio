Motion Graphics · Case Study

Eyes Wide Shut

►

Introduction

Eyes Wide Shut is a 10-second product visualization of a Lamy fountain pen. I built the scene in Blender and finished it in After Effects. The pen moves across a red stage under a single spotlight, past my lion emblem and my name set in 3D letters. It was my first full project in Blender, from the first sketch to the final video.

  • Project type: Course project, Motion Graphics 3 (DSN1688), Algonquin College, Fall 2026
  • Role: Solo designer and animator
  • Category: Product visualization / 3D motion
  • Programs: Blender, Adobe After Effects
  • Product model: A ready-made 3D model of a Lamy fountain pen
  • Audio: From the Pixabay collection

The Challenge

The brief asked for a product visualization in Blender: choose a product, plan it, light it, and animate one hero move, such as a turntable, a push-in, an orbit, a light sweep, or a reveal. Before building anything, we made a moodboard and a storyboard and presented them to a small group in class for feedback.

My own challenge was the camera and the light. A fountain pen is thin, dark, and glossy. If the light is wrong, it disappears into the background. If the camera is wrong, it looks like a flat stick. Every choice had to help the pen read as a real object in a few seconds.

Constraints: one hero move, a storyboard review before the build, and a finished video file by the Week 09 deadline.

The Process

Planning. My moodboard collected the product, references for materials and lighting, and the colour direction: a deep red stage, black around it, and one strong spotlight. My storyboard sketched the key moments of the move.

Feedback. I presented both to my storyboard group and my instructor. The response was very good. My instructor liked both the moodboard and the sketches, so I kept the direction and went straight into building.

Building. In Blender, I placed the Lamy pen model on the stage and built my lion emblem and my name as extruded 3D shapes behind it. Then I set up the spotlight and the camera and animated the move.

Rendering. I rendered the animation from Blender as a sequence of still images instead of one video file. Each frame is saved on its own, so if a render stops halfway, nothing finished is lost. I brought the sequence into After Effects, put it together with the music, and exported the final video.

The Solution

The finished piece keeps everything simple so the pen can lead. The red stage gives it a strong, warm ground. The spotlight picks out its shape and leaves the rest in shadow. My emblem and my name stay still while the pen moves, so the eye always knows what to follow.

The title came from the light. As I set up the deep red stage and the single spotlight, the colours took me back to watching Eyes Wide Shut by Stanley Kubrick, one of the great directors in the history of cinema. His film is built from rich reds, deep shadows, and pools of light, and my scene had found its way into the same mood. So I named the piece after it.

A fountain pen is the tool you sign your name with. Here it moves past my name as if it has just written it. I like that: the name is mine, but the hand that writes is never quite my own.

The Outcome

The moodboard and storyboard were approved without major changes, and the final video was finished as an MP4 for the November 6 deadline.

Reflection & Learning

Skills I strengthened: camera animation and lighting in Blender, rendering image sequences, and moving a render from Blender into After Effects for the final edit.

What I’d do differently: I’d model the pen myself instead of using a ready-made model, so every part of the product is my own work. I’d also test more lighting setups early, because the light was what decided whether the pen looked real or flat.

What I learned about myself: The hardest part was not building the objects. It was learning to look through the camera and to place the light. In 3D, you have to decide what the viewer sees and what stays in the dark. That is where the image is really made.

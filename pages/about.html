About

I do not know precisely how to describe the image that takes shape before me in the mirror and trembles back toward my gaze. It is not the portrait that held Dorian Gray—or the ill-fated Oscar Wilde himself—spellbound, until it grew so dreadful that it forced him to bow before it and suffer a tragic fall. Nor is it the reflection that drew Narcissus into the depths of his own vanity and self-worship. What I see in the mirror is the figure of a man standing atop the colossal watchtowers of ancient Constantinople, one who has accepted the fate of both himself and his ancestral homeland, yet still refuses to lower his head in surrender. And at the same time, I see a resemblance to that ancient blacksmith of epic legend, who, in a final attempt to save his last hope—the last fruit of his life, his youngest son—storms into the hall of the Serpent-King with righteous fury.

Background

Education

As an independent visual artist, I have spent a considerable part of my life searching for the most direct path toward building a sustainable life of my own—from pursuing different fields of study to experiencing life in different lands. A brief account of my educational journey and achievements is as follows:

  • High School Diploma — Medical Science School
  • Hotel Management Diploma
  • Academic Study of Foreign Languages — German, French, and Turkish, in Iran
  • English for Academic Purposes (EAP) — Algonquin College, with Honours
  • Design Foundation Certificate — Algonquin College, with Honours
  • Graphic Design Diploma — Algonquin College (forthcoming)

Focus

My focus lies upon an ocean of turbulent waves—composed of infinitesimally small particles that surround the world we inhabit, yet remain hidden from the sight of us humans, confined as we are within the limits of perception, for an indescribable eternity. These are precisely the places where the gods reside, calling us toward themselves—to join them, to intermingle with them, and ultimately to become one with them. My primary specialization is the creation of entirely imagined visual compositions. Consequently, the principal digital tools I work with are Adobe Illustrator and Adobe After Effects. I also possess a solid command of traditional drawing practices rooted in the methods of earlier generations, as well as entirely digital approaches built upon artificial intelligence. I have therefore drawn upon all of these tools and disciplines in creating this online collection—as a mirror intended to reflect my true identity: the man behind the mask, the questioning man.

Influence

As a questioning artist, throughout the relatively brief span of my life, I have found myself under the influence of countless ideas and schools of thought. Among them, within the realm of modern painting, I can name Cubism and Surrealism. Yet it should hardly come as a surprise that, before any other artistic inclination, I—like so many others—became captivated by my encounters with the art of Classical and Renaissance European painting. One cannot speak of painting while entirely overlooking the art of sculpture, nor can one speak of sculpture without invoking the great sculptors who gave form to the astonishing and tangible image of the human being. Michelangelo and Rodin, to my mind, stand among the most renowned and unforgettable names in the brief history of humanity. Within the vast realm of literature into which I have ventured thus far, I can point to Russian Realism, and to masters such as Dostoevsky and Tolstoy—figures who opened before me a world at once profoundly terrifying and filled with profound moral lessons. Yet this particular literary tradition constitutes only a small fragment of the worlds discovered by my curious eyes. It would be impossible to overlook the tremendous blows delivered by the turbulent waves unleashed through the volcanic eruptions of the inner worlds of such figures as Franz Kafka, Sadegh Hedayat, Gabriel García Márquez, Orhan Pamuk, Yukio Mishima, and so many other influential literary voices whose worlds have crashed upon me with formidable force. I must then turn to the philosophers and profound thinkers who, more than any others, challenged my identity and intellectual foundations, only to reconstruct them anew: Socrates, Omar Khayyam, Niccolò Machiavelli, Friedrich Nietzsche, Søren Kierkegaard, Albert Camus, and Yuval Noah Harari. And finally, almost as a litany of names, I must acknowledge those wise human beings who illuminated my world with the boundlessness of their knowledge: great minds such as Sigmund Freud, Émile Durkheim, and Carl Jung.
